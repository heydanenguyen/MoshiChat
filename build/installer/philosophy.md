# Tender Geometry

A movement for small, warm objects made with exacting care. Tender Geometry holds that friendliness is not a
loosening of rigour but its most generous form: every soft curve is drawn from a circle whose centre was chosen,
every blush of colour sits on a grid that was measured. The work should feel effortless to the eye and be,
underneath, the product of painstaking calibration — the kind of meticulously crafted surface that only
master-level execution makes look this simple.

Space is the first material. Figures are few and they are given room: a single pair of characters, a single
speech bubble, a single word. Negative space is not empty but held — a pale field that behaves like light in a
studio, brighter near the subject and falling away toward the edges. Compositions are vertical and quiet, with
the eye travelling from a whispered marker at the top, to the figure at the centre of gravity, to a grounded
name at the base. Nothing crowds the frame; every element keeps a margin as if placed with tweezers.

Colour arrives in two registers. The ground is a soft mesh of pastel fields — lilac, blush, peach, sky —
blended so gently that no seam can be found. Against it, the figures are pure, flat, saturated shapes with no
gradients and no outlines, their faces drawn in a single near-black ink. The contrast between the breathing
ground and the confident flat forms is the whole drama; it must be tuned by hand until the figures feel lit
from within, the result of deep expertise rather than a filter.

Rhythm comes from patient repetition. A faint lattice of dots, spaced with clinical regularity, sits beneath
the colour like graph paper under a watercolour. Celebration is expressed not by chaos but by an ordered
scatter — small pieces of colour arranged along a spiral, each one a perfect primitive: a circle, a rounded
bar, a triangle. The family of characters, when it appears, stands in a strict row at a strict interval, a
specimen plate of good moods.

Type is sparse and purposeful. One word may be large and heavy, set in a round geometric sans with tight
tracking, speaking in the figures' own voice. Everything else is small: a monospaced index number, a lowercase
caption, set with generous letter-spacing and reduced opacity so it reads as annotation, never as copy. Text is
a visual accent that anchors what the image already says.

The final work must look labored over: optically centred rather than mathematically centred, shadows that are
barely there, bubbles whose tails meet their bodies without a visible joint, every edge crisp at the pixel. It
should feel like a page from a beautifully printed field guide to kindness — precise, affectionate, and made by
someone at the very top of their craft.
